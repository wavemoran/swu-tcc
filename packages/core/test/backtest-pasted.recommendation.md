## Recommendation

**Ship paste mode for final-round ID decisions, with the warnings below.** Across 8 real events (103 players near the cut, 4 input shapes, 3 tiebreaker models) the one claim that must never be wrong was never wrong: every `certain` and `impossible` scenario was right (0 errors among 113 to 189 proven scenarios per input shape, checked against the real result and against all three counterfactual results). The dangerous error, "pasted mode says the draw is safe (>= 90%) but a draw would have missed the cut", happened 0 times for model A, 0 to 1 for model C (the default) and 2 for model B; the full-data engine had 0. Nobody who actually drew was hurt by any variant.

**Default model: C** (players tied on points are ordered by current rank with a coin-flip margin). Brier scores, counterfactual, all scenarios / draw scenario only, posted pairings: full data 0.0289 / 0.0428; A 0.0426 / 0.0483; B 0.0300 / 0.0553; C 0.0451 / 0.0444. B is the best calibrated overall and nearly matches full data on win and loss scenarios, but it is overconfident on the draw scenario (two 93% safe calls where full data said 70 to 77%, one of them a player who then missed the cut). C is the only pasted model whose draw scenario is about as well calibrated as full data (0.0444 vs 0.0428 posted; 0.0515 vs 0.0439 predicted), and the draw scenario is the number that matters for an ID. A is safe but the least sharp. The differences between models rest on a handful of players; re-measure when more events are captured. B stays available as `variant: 'B'` if the UI wants a sharper win/loss number.

**Accuracy lost versus full data.** For the default model, verdict status matches full mode on 91% (posted) and 87% (predicted) of the three scenarios per player; the mean absolute probability difference on the draw scenario is 0.027 (posted) and 0.031 (predicted), and about 0.05 to 0.06 on win and loss. The game record column makes almost no difference (it only moves GW), so do not require it. Posted pairings help: 124 vs 76 scenarios become provable from points, and the rematch-avoidance error disappears. Ask for them when the player can paste them.

**Warn in the UI when:**

1. The draw scenario is not `certain`, that is, it depends on tiebreakers. Every wrong call in the backtest was of this kind.
2. The draw scenario is estimated and below 95%. In the backtest every estimated draw at 95% or more was right (45 for C), while every dangerous call was between 92% and 94%. The engine enforces this: in paste mode an estimated draw needs 0.95 (`PASTED_ID_SAFE_THRESHOLD`) to be ID SAFE, and 90% to 95% comes out as BUBBLE; a backtest assertion checks that the default variant makes no dangerous call at that bar.
3. Pairings are predicted rather than posted (rematch avoidance cannot be modeled, so a table that decides the cut may differ).
4. More than one round remains: the numbers are a rough points-only estimate and the UI should say to paste again before the final round.

**Assumptions text for the UI (paste mode, final round):**

> Calculated from the standings you pasted, not the full match history. We can see points and the tiebreaker percentages (OMW, OGW) as they are now, but not who played whom, so we cannot recompute them after the last round. Everything is worked out from match points: other tables are 50/50 (a table where both players could draw into the cut also draws half the time), and players who end level with you on points are ranked by their current standing with a margin of error. "Locked" and "Out" are proven from points alone and are exact. "ID safe" and the percentages are estimates: where your finish depends on a tiebreaker, treat anything under 95% as a gamble. [If pairings were not posted:] Final-round pairings are predicted 1 v 2, 3 v 4, and so on; real pairings avoid rematches, which we cannot see, so a table or two may differ. Paste the posted pairings for a sharper answer.
